PV candidate survey: diatomic molecules and ions

We look for diatomic molecules and singly charged ions whose ground state suits nuclear-spin-dependent parity-violation (NSD-PV) measurements, where a rotational interval 2B(N+1) nearly matches the hyperfine interval hf = (I+½)|bF|. Each row is one isotopologue. By default, the table shows only rows whose ground state is surely or probably 2Σ; tick the other 2Σ? boxes to see the rest. δ0 is the zero-field gap between opposite-parity levels of equal F in N = 0 and N = 1. δmin is the smallest such gap over N ≤ 7. Both use exact levels where c or γ is known (or S ≥ 1), and 2B(N+1) − hf otherwise. Each ± is the 1σ uncertainty from B and bF (see Methods). The N pair boxes (2↔3 means N = 2 and N = 3) restrict δmin and Bc to those pairs. "As-if-Σ" rows have an unknown ground state, so their constants assume the unpaired electron occupies a σ orbital. The DFT-state columns give the state our DFT found and the energies (cm⁻¹) of the other states, relative to it; they are not a ground-state assignment. Ground states in grey italics with ? are estimated by analogy with another molecule, not taken from a source on this one.

Methods

Constants. Each B and bF carries its source: measured; scaled from a measured or calculated isotopologue of the same molecule; calculated in the literature; our CC; our DFT; or our estimate. Scaling between isotopologues (reduced mass for B, nuclear g-factor for bF) is as accurate as the parent value, so a value scaled from a calculation counts as a calculation. Our estimates either rescale another molecule of the same charge (approximate) or start from atomic radii or the free-ion contact interaction (zero order, crude). Where several values exist, we use the most direct: measured first, estimated last.

DFT. We use ORCA 6.1.1 with all-electron scalar-relativistic Hamiltonians. An unrestricted Kohn–Sham scan of the bond length gives re and Be; at re, an X2C calculation (decontracted x2c-TZVPall-s basis, finite nucleus, picture-change corrected) gives bF and c. A DKH2 g-tensor calculation gives Δg⊥, and hence γ = −2BΔg⊥ (Curl relation). We chose the functional for each class of molecule (B3LYP, PBE0 with 40% exact exchange, or BP86) by benchmarking against measured molecules. The class's RMS error sets the bF uncertainty (25% for molecules outside every benchmarked class), and the gaps use at least 20%. The x2c basis sets stop at Rn, so for Ra, Ac and Th we use Dyall's uncontracted all-electron v3z basis.

Coupled cluster. For GaCl+ and GaBr+, we also compute bF with relativistic coupled cluster (four-component Dirac–Coulomb CCSD(T) in DIRAC 26.1, uncontracted Dyall v2z basis, outer core correlated). Finite-field energy differences give A∥, and bF = A∥ − 2c/3 with c from our DFT; a CCSD(T) scan of the bond length gives B0 (first-order Dunham, Be − αe/2). At this level, CC underestimates the measured A∥ of the isoelectronic neutrals ZnCl and ZnBr by 9–10% (our DFT by 3%), so we scale each cation's A∥ and B0 by the measured/CC ratio of its Zn-halide partner. The largest uncertainty item is whether that correction carries over from the neutral to the cation, and we set it at half the correction. Where a CC value exists, the gaps use it instead of DFT, and the table lists both. CC values appear once these runs finish.

Estimates. Without a measured or calculated value, we estimate the bond length from related molecules of the same charge, scaled by covalent radii (Pyykkö–Atsumi), or from atomic radii alone when no related molecule is known. For bF, we scale the Fermi contact interaction of a related molecule, or of the free ion, to the target nucleus. Leave-one-out tests on molecules with known values set the uncertainties, and the gaps use at least 50% for bF. Estimates are placeholders until a measurement or calculation exists.

Uncertainties. δ0 and δmin follow one rule, to first order in B and bF: σ² = [2(N+1)σB]² + [(hf/|bF|)σbF]². We use a value's stated uncertainty where it has one. Without one, a measured value, or one scaled from a measured isotopologue, counts as exact, and a gap with no uncertainty at all shows no ±; any other value takes 5% on B and 20% on bF. The floors above still apply to bF from our DFT and from estimates. σ leaves out c, γ and model error. When σ(δmin) exceeds 2B, the N of closest approach is itself unknown, so the ± is only a scale.

Levels and crossings. For 2Σ molecules with enough known constants, we diagonalize the effective Hamiltonian (case (b) basis |N G F MF⟩, with γ, bF, c and eQq) in magnetic fields up to 200 mT. The level plots and crossings show only same-MF, opposite-parity pairs whose PV matrix element |⟨a|n·(I×S)|b⟩|/I exceeds 0.02. The basis stops at N = 11, so the pairs shown sit well inside it; crossings that move by more than 1 G at N = 13 are left out of Bc.
